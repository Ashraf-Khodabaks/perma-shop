What’s in the finish?

The jackets, shoes and trousers on this site share one trick: water and stains slide off them. That trick comes from a family of chemicals called PFAS.

PFAS in one paragraph

PFAS (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances) are thousands of human-made chemicals. They repel water, grease and dirt. That is why they show up in rain jackets, stain-resistant fabric, non-stick pans, food packaging and firefighting foam.

Why they’re called forever chemicals

The bond between carbon and fluorine is one of the strongest in organic chemistry. Nature has no easy way to break it, so many PFAS stay in water, soil and living bodies for decades or longer.

How a jacket ends up in the water

A finish doesn’t stay on the fabric forever. Washing and wear loosen it, and it travels down the drain. Wastewater treatment plants are not designed to remove PFAS, so they pass through to rivers and the sea. PFAS have been found in drinking water sources, in wildlife and in the blood of people all over the world.

Why people are worried

Some PFAS have been linked to higher cholesterol, a weaker response to vaccines in children, and effects on the thyroid and liver. In 2023 the International Agency for Research on Cancer classified PFOA as carcinogenic to humans and PFOS as possibly carcinogenic.

Most PFAS have never been studied one by one. Because there are so many, scientists and regulators increasingly look at them as a group.

What Europe is doing

In 2023, Denmark, Germany, the Netherlands, Norway and Sweden sent the European Chemicals Agency a proposal to restrict PFAS as a group. It is still being evaluated, so check ECHA for the latest status.

Not every waterproof jacket contains PFAS

PFAS-free finishes exist for many everyday uses. They can perform differently, especially in extreme weather. For a commute in the rain, you probably need less than the label promises.

What you can do

  • Look for “PFAS-free” on the label, and ask brands when it isn’t there.
  • Choose water repellency only where you really need it.
  • Wash less, and skip re-proofing sprays that contain fluorinated chemicals.
  • Repair and reuse. A longer life for a garment means fewer new finishes.
